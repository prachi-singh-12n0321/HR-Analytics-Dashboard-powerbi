# HR Analytics Dashboard

## Project Overview

This project analyzes employee attrition patterns using Power BI. The goal was to identify workforce trends, understand factors contributing to employee turnover, and provide insights that can support employee retention strategies.

---

## Business Problem

Employee attrition can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity, and impact organizational performance. HR teams need visibility into attrition trends across departments, job roles, age groups, and employee tenure to make informed workforce decisions.

## Key Insights

### 1. Employees aged 25–34 recorded the highest attrition

The 25–34 age group accounted for the largest number of employees leaving the organization, indicating higher turnover among early-career professionals.

### 2. R&D department experienced the highest attrition

Among all departments, Research & Development showed the greatest employee attrition, highlighting a potential area for retention-focused initiatives.

### 3. Laboratory Technicians had the highest attrition among job roles

The Laboratory Technician role reported the largest number of employees leaving the organization, followed by Sales Executive and Research Scientist positions.

### 4. Male employees represented the majority of attrition cases

Male employees accounted for approximately 63% of total attrition, while female employees accounted for around 37%.

### 5. Attrition was highest during the early years of employment

Most employee departures occurred within the first few years of joining the company, suggesting the importance of onboarding, engagement, and early-career retention programs.
